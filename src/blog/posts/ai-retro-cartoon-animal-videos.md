## Step-by-step

### Step 1: Plan your video with the master prompt

Copy the master prompt provided above and paste it into a fresh chat in Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini. The prompt acts as your scriptwriter and production assistant, asking you questions one step at a time.

You will choose your hero animal (such as a wolf or fox), video format, and duration. For vertical formats like Shorts or Reels, select a 9:16 aspect ratio. Pick a topic from the generated viral list—such as energy drinks, breakfast cereals, or credit cards—or enter your own. The prompt will generate verified historical facts and output a timed script split into 4-second and 6-second scenes.

### Step 2: Create reference sheets in Gemini

The master prompt produces exact image prompts for Gemini (Nano Banana). These prompts define your suited hero, the gullible buddy character, flashback figures, the fictional product lookalike, and your portal object (such as a retro television or pocket watch).

Generate these character turnaround sheets and prop references on plain backgrounds. Save each image to your computer, as you will feed them directly into your animation tool as visual ingredients to keep every character consistent across different angles.

### Step 3: Generate scene images in Google Flow

Open Google Flow in Agent mode and upload the reference sheets you created in Gemini. The master prompt gives you a single consolidated block of text containing every scene prompt alongside a final renaming command.

Paste this entire block into a single Agent mode message. Google Flow will generate every scene image according to your chosen style: warm, ink-lined 1940s color animation for the present day, and sepia or black-and-white for the flashback scenes. Once generated, the agent will automatically rename every file in order so they match your script.

### Step 4: Animate scenes within daily limits

Google Flow charges 7 credits for a 4-second clip and 10 credits for a 6-second clip. The master prompt automatically groups your scenes into daily batches totaling 50 credits or fewer, allowing you to animate the entire project on a free tier without paying for an upgrade.

Paste the animation instructions for your first batch into Google Flow Agent mode. The prompt specifies camera movements, mouth movements, and character gestures. Make sure to follow the portal transition instructions: the camera pushes close into the portal object before the color drains for the flashback, then pulls back out when the story returns to the present.

### Step 5: Generate audio and music in ElevenLabs

Head over to ElevenLabs to set up character voices using the Voice Design descriptions from your plan. Create a calm, low baritone for the hero mascot and a warm, slightly nervous tone for the buddy.

Use ElevenLabs Text to Dialogue to generate the full script. The master prompt formats every line with emotional audio tags and spells out numbers to prevent awkward pronunciation. Generate sound effects for your portal device, such as TV static or a ticking clock, along with subtle noir jazz for the present and vintage period music for the flashback.

### Step 6: Assemble the edit in CapCut

Open CapCut and import your animated clips, dialogue tracks, sound effects, and music. Lay down the dialogue on your timeline first to establish the pacing.

Align each animated clip to its corresponding spoken line. Cut silent reaction clips into 4-second gaps to let the humor land. Place your portal sound effects right on the cut between color and black-and-white, balance the background music underneath the dialogue, and export your finished video.

## Tips for better results

* **Track your daily credit budget:** Keep your animation batches at or below 50 credits per day. Leaving a few credits unused each day gives you room for retakes without stalling production.
* **Keep visual ingredients consistent:** Never change the character descriptions between scenes. Rely on your uploaded reference sheets so the characters keep identical outfits, colors, and proportions.
* **Enforce the portal rule:** Ensure the portal object appears in the opening scene, stays in the hero's hand during the trigger, fills the entire frame during the transition, and reappears when exiting the flashback.
* **Respect word limits:** Stick to a maximum of 8 words for 4-second clips and 12 words for 6-second clips. This keeps the animation natural and prevents rushed voice lines.

## FAQ

### Can I make this entire video without paying for tools?

### Why are the flashback scenes black-and-white?
The contrast between the full-color present and the monochrome flashback clearly separates the modern dialogue from the historical breakdown. It also keeps the focus on the product, which is the only colored item inside the flashback.

### Do I need to write my own prompts for the scenes?
No. The master prompt writes every scene prompt, animation instruction, character turnaround description, and voice setting for you based on the choices you make during the planning step.
