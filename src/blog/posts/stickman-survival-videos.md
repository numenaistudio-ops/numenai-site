In this guide, you will learn how to create your own animated stickman survival video from start to finish. Stickman apocalypse stories have gained massive traction on YouTube because simple visual characters paired with high-stakes tension keep viewers hooked from the very first second.

The best part is that you do not need traditional drawing skills or expensive animation software to pull this off. By using a single structured master prompt alongside a set of free web tools, you can handle everything from plot outlines and visual character consistency to movement, voice acting, and final editing.

## What you'll make

- A complete 2D animated stickman survival story, such as a zombie apocalypse scenario, tailored for vertical YouTube Shorts or standard widescreen videos.
- Character reference sheets that keep your stickman heroes and zombie threats visually consistent across every shot.
- Clean 2D background environments and scene images generated from detailed text prompts.
- Short, animated video clips created through image-to-video generation with natural motion and camera movement.
- A timed voiceover track and a polished final video edit complete with sound effects and a matching thumbnail.

## What you need

- Claude AI: Used to run the master prompt, organize the story beats, generate character descriptions, and write all visual prompts and narration scripts.
- Google Gemini: Used to generate clean 2D stickman character sheets and individual scene images based on the written prompts.
- Google Flow: Used for image-to-video animation to turn your still scene images into moving video clips.
- ElevenLabs: Used to generate a natural, cinematic voiceover track from your scene-by-scene script.
- CapCut: Used to assemble the animated video clips, align your voiceover audio, and finalize the complete project.

## Step-by-step

### Step 1: Plan your story and format with Claude AI

Begin by copying the master prompt displayed directly above this article and pasting it into Claude AI. The prompt directs Claude to act as your production director and work with you through clear, numbered stages.

Claude will first ask you to set your target video duration, such as a sixty-second Short or a multi-minute video, along with your chosen aspect ratio (9:16 vertical or 16:9 widescreen) and narration tone. Once you provide those details, you can upload reference images or ask Claude for style options. From there, Claude pitches five original story ideas complete with catchy titles, opening hooks, and plot twists. Pick your favorite concept, and Claude will calculate the required scene count and output a structured story outline.

### Step 2: Build consistent character sheets

Visual consistency is critical when telling an animated story. Once your story outline is approved, Claude provides a character consistency guide. This includes fixed appearance lock descriptions for your main character, companions, and zombie types.

Claude also writes specialized character sheet image prompts. Copy these prompts and paste them into Google Gemini to generate full-body turnarounds—showing front, three-quarter, side, and back views—along with distinct facial expressions on a plain background. Save these turnaround images to your computer. You will reference them throughout the rest of your production to ensure your stickman looks identical in every single scene.

### Step 3: Generate scene images in Google Gemini

Next, Claude generates complete scene image prompts for every beat in your outline. Each prompt includes the established style lock, the character appearance lock, specific body poses, camera angles, and background lighting.

Take each scene prompt into Google Gemini to generate your visual backgrounds and character action shots. Review each image carefully to confirm that clothing, held items, environmental details, and injuries stay continuous from one moment to the next. Download every finished frame in order.

### Step 4: Animate scenes with Google Flow

Once your still images are ready, Claude generates tailored video prompts for each scene. These prompts define the exact duration of each shot (typically five to eight seconds), the starting and ending action, character movements, environmental effects like rain or smoke, and camera motions like slow pushes or tracking shots.

Open Google Flow and upload your still image using the image-to-video feature. Paste the matching video prompt from Claude to guide the motion. Google Flow will generate a moving clip that preserves the characters and layout from your Gemini still. Download each clip and keep your files organized by scene number.

### Step 5: Generate the voiceover with ElevenLabs

Claude provides a complete voiceover script matched to the timing of your video clips, written at approximately two and a half words per second. The script includes emotional cues in brackets, such as whispering or panicked tones, to guide the performance.

Head over to ElevenLabs, select a voice that fits a cinematic survival atmosphere, and paste your script. Generate the audio track and download it as an audio file. You can generate lines scene by scene or paste the full continuous script block provided at the end of the Claude output.

### Step 6: Create your thumbnail and edit in CapCut

Claude concludes the master prompt process by providing thumbnail concepts and a dedicated thumbnail image prompt. Generate the thumbnail in Google Gemini, making sure it features expressive characters, clear focal contrast, and bold rendered text.

Finally, open CapCut and import all of your animated video clips from Google Flow and your voiceover audio from ElevenLabs. Place the clips on your timeline in order, align them with the spoken narration, trim any excess frames, and add ambient sound effects to enhance the tension. Once everything plays smoothly, export your finished video.

## Tips for better results

- Keep appearance locks identical: Never shorten or rephrase the character appearance blocks generated by Claude; pasting the exact wording into Google Gemini ensures your stickman stays visually consistent.
- Mind the scene timing: Aim for individual clip durations between five and eight seconds. This keeps the pacing brisk and prevents viewer fatigue.
- Check visual continuity: Verify that props, held weapons, clothing tears, and lighting directions carry over naturally between consecutive scene prompts before generating motion.
- Guide audio emotion: Pay close attention to the bracketed directions in the script so the voiceover inflection matches the danger occurring on screen.

## FAQ

### Do I need drawing or animation experience to make these videos?
No, you do not need any drawing or technical animation background. The master prompt writes all the necessary visual descriptions, and the AI tools create the artwork and movement for you.

### How do I make sure my stickman looks the same in every clip?
The master prompt generates fixed appearance locks and turnaround character sheets. By using these same exact descriptive locks and reference frames across every image prompt, your characters remain identical.

### Can I use this workflow for formats other than YouTube Shorts?
Yes. In the very first step of the master prompt, you can choose between a 9:16 vertical aspect ratio for Shorts or a 16:9 landscape aspect ratio for standard widescreen videos.
