In this guide, you will learn how to create your own animated stickman videos from start to finish without paying for animation software or subscriptions. Using a structured five-tool pipeline, you can turn a simple story concept into a fully voiced, animated video ready to share.

Stickman storytelling has become an increasingly popular format for short-form content because it is clean, relatable, and easy for audiences to follow. With modern AI tools, you no longer need traditional drawing skills to produce consistent characters, fluid motion, and crisp voiceovers.

## What you'll make

* A complete 9:16 vertical short-form story structured for high audience retention.
* Consistent 2D vector stickman characters featuring circle heads, cream eyes, and signature color garments.
* Animated scene clips converted directly from static vector image frames.
* A fully edited final video with synchronized voiceover narration and tight cuts.

## What you need

* Claude AI: Used to plan your narrative hooks, script breakdowns, character anchor sheets, and image prompts.
* Google Gemini: Used to generate the flat-shaded, low-key 2D vector stick-figure scene frames.
* Google Flow: Used for image-to-video motion rendering to bring static vector frames to life.
* ElevenLabs: Used to generate realistic, clear voiceover narration from your script.
* CapCut: Used for clip assembly, audio synchronization, and final video editing.

## Step-by-step

### Step 1: Generate your production plan in Claude AI

Begin by copying the master prompt displayed above this article. Open Claude AI and paste the prompt to generate your entire production pipeline in one structured system. Claude will produce your story hooks, full script, character design sheets, scene breakdowns, text-to-image prompts, motion controls, and voiceover scripts.

By running this single master prompt, you avoid guessing what happens next in your story. The prompt organizes your narrative specifically for a 9:16 short-form layout, ensuring every second serves a clear storytelling purpose.

### Step 2: Establish your character design anchors

Maintaining character consistency across multiple scenes is critical for animation. Review the character design sheets created by Claude. The workflow relies on flat-shaded, low-key 2D vector stickmen defined by clear identity anchors: round circle heads, cream eyes, and signature color garments.

These locked identity anchors ensure your character remains recognizable from the first frame to the last. Keep these descriptions handy, as they will serve as the foundation for every scene you generate next.

### Step 3: Generate scene frames with Google Gemini

Take the scene prompts generated by Claude and bring them into Google Gemini. Here, you will generate clean, static vector frames for every beat of your story.

Gemini will interpret the prompt details to build the low-key 2D vector style, keeping the background simple so your stickman character remains the focal point. Save each approved image frame to your computer in sequential order to keep your project organized.

### Step 4: Render motion with Google Flow

Once your static frames are ready, open Google Flow to convert those 2D images into animated video clips. Google Flow takes your static vector art and applies fluid motion based on the motion controls outlined in your Claude production plan.

Upload each scene frame one by one, input the corresponding motion prompt, and render the resulting video clips. This transforms your flat illustrations into expressive, moving scenes without manual keyframing.

### Step 5: Create a matching thumbnail

Before moving into video assembly, create a compelling thumbnail frame for your project. You can generate or select a strong visual frame that captures the core conflict or curiosity of your story.

A clear, bold frame showing your signature stickman character helps set viewer expectations and gives your project a clean presentation when published.

### Step 6: Generate your voiceover in ElevenLabs

Open ElevenLabs to create the narration for your animation. Copy the voiceover script provided in your initial Claude output and paste it into the voice generator.

Choose a natural, clear voice profile that matches the pacing of your short-form script. Once rendered, download the audio track to pair with your visual assets in the editing stage.

### Step 7: Assemble and edit in CapCut

Open CapCut and start a new project set to a 9:16 vertical aspect ratio. Import your animated video clips from Google Flow along with your ElevenLabs voiceover track.

Line up your video clips on the timeline so they match the timing of the spoken narration. Trim any unnecessary pauses to create snappy, fast-paced cutdowns that maintain engagement. Once the visual cuts and audio tracks are aligned, export your finished video.

## Tips for better results

* Keep identity anchors consistent: Always include the circle head, cream eyes, and signature garment colors in every visual prompt to prevent character drift.

* Mind your pacing: Trim dead space between sentences in CapCut to keep your short-form animation moving forward smoothly.
* Match motion to the scene: Keep motion prompts focused on simple, natural movements so the vector stickman figures remain clear and readable.
