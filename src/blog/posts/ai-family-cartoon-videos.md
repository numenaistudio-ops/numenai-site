## Step-by-step

### Step 1: Generate your story and script in Claude AI
Start by copying the master prompt provided directly above this article. Open Claude AI, paste the prompt into a new chat, and send it. Claude will act as your showrunner and pitch several story concepts centered on relatable family moments.

Review the suggestions and pick the concept you like best, or pitch your own adjustment. Claude will then produce a full scene-by-scene script breakdown. This includes the setting, camera framing, exact dialogue lines, and character expressions for each sketch. Read through the breakdown and approve it so your dialogue and scene actions remain locked for the rest of the workflow.

### Step 2: Create your image prompts for each scene
Once your script is approved, Claude will produce specific first-frame image prompts formatted for image generation in Google Gemini. Each prompt includes detailed physical descriptors for the baby, Mama, and Papa to ensure strong family resemblance, such as matching skin tones and hair textures.

Make sure to review each prompt block. Every sketch is designed as a continuous take, meaning all characters and props appearing in that particular scene must be visible in the first-frame prompt. This prevents objects or characters from popping into the scene unexpectedly during animation.

### Step 3: Generate the first-frame scenes in Google Gemini
Head over to Google Gemini to begin generating your images. Paste the first-frame prompt for Scene 1 into the generator, ensuring your aspect ratio is set to 9:16 vertical.

To keep your characters consistent across all scenes, generate clear character references early. You can generate a clean close-up of your baby character first, followed by Mama and Papa. Use these approved images as reference inputs when generating the remaining scenes. Download each high-resolution first-frame image once you are satisfied with the composition and lighting.

### Step 4: Animate your scenes in Google Flow
Open Google Flow to turn your still images into motion. For each scene, select the image-to-video mode and upload your corresponding first-frame image. Then, paste the matching video prompt generated by Claude in Phase 4 of the workflow.

Google Flow, using tools like Omni 1.1 Flash, will interpret the timeline cues and animate the action across a ten-second continuous take. Pay attention to the camera directions and dialogue timings provided in the prompt. Generate each clip one by one, review the movement and expressions, and download all seven completed video clips to your computer.

### Step 5: Generate a matching video thumbnail
Before heading to the editing suite, use the publishing pack section generated by your master prompt to create a thumbnail. Claude provides dedicated prompts for 16:9 horizontal YouTube thumbnails as well as 9:16 vertical covers for Reels and TikTok.

Generate your cover image using Google Gemini. The prompt instructs the model to create a full-bleed, expressive composition with bold, readable text rendered directly inside the image, saving you extra editing steps later.

### Step 6: Assemble and edit in Wondershare Filmora
Launch Wondershare Filmora and create a vertical 9:16 project. Import your downloaded video clips from Google Flow and arrange them chronologically on your timeline.

Trim any slight pauses at the start or end of the clips to keep the comedic timing brisk. Because each sketch is designed as a self-contained beat, use simple straight cuts or gentle transitions between scenes. Finally, layer in a soft background music track—such as a gentle piano or lo-fi bed—and place subtle sound effects for punchlines, giggles, or foot stomps. When everything plays smoothly, export your finished video.

## Tips for better results

* **Keep dialogue brief:** Babies speak best in short bursts like "Mama!", "No.", or "Shh." Limiting speech to a few words per clip ensures cleaner lip-syncing and better cartoon timing.
* **Maintain family resemblance:** Always verify that your image prompts include shared traits between the baby and parents, such as identical skin tones, hair colors, and eye shapes.
* **Frame all props in frame one:** Ensure every prop used in a sketch—such as a plush bunny, a blanket, or a snack bag—is visible in the opening image so the video generator handles it correctly.
* **Work within free limits:** Keep an eye on daily generation credits and model quotas across Google Gemini and Google Flow, adjusting your batch generations accordingly.

## FAQ

### Can I use these AI tools without paying for subscriptions?
Yes, you can follow this workflow using the free tiers of Claude AI, Google Gemini, and Google Flow. Keep in mind that available credits, generation limits, and specific model access may vary over time.

### Why does the master prompt keep dialogue locked after Phase 2?
Locking the dialogue ensures that character lines, audio cues, and lip-sync timings remain perfectly synchronized between your text script, image frames, and final video prompts.

### What should I do if the motion in Google Flow feels rushed?
If an animated action feels too fast, trim non-essential dialogue or simplify the secondary movements in your prompt, but keep the scene as a single continuous ten-second take.
